\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Overview of the MR-based framework}
\label{res:analysis-set}

We propose a Mendelian randomization (MR) framework to assess the causal effect of chromatin accessibility at a candidate peak on the expression of a nearby gene within a given cell type (Fig.~1A). For each peak--gene pair, we use chromatin accessibility QTL (caQTL) and expression QTL (eQTL) summary statistics for \(J\) single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). Let \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\hat{\gamma}_1,\ldots,\hat{\gamma}_J)^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}=(\hat{\Gamma}_1,\ldots,\hat{\Gamma}_J)^{\mathsf T}\) denote the estimated SNP associations with peak accessibility and gene expression, respectively. Inverse-variance weighting (IVW) and MR-Egger use the same regression model:
\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}
= \alpha_0\boldsymbol{1}_J + \beta\hat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
  + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
\sim \mathcal{N}_J(\boldsymbol{0},\phi\boldsymbol{\Sigma}).
\]
\noindent Here, \(\beta\) represents the causal effect of peak accessibility on gene expression under the MR assumptions. IVW fixes the intercept \(\alpha_0\) at zero, whereas MR-Egger estimates it to allow for directional pleiotropy. The covariance matrix \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\) accounts for the standard errors of the eQTL estimates and linkage disequilibrium among SNPs estimated from a reference panel. The dispersion parameter \(\phi\) allows for multiplicative overdispersion.

\noindent Summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) uses the lead SNP, defined as the SNP with the smallest caQTL \(p\)-value for the peak. Denoting this SNP by \(*\), the SMR estimate is \(\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{SMR}}=\hat{\Gamma}_*/\hat{\gamma}_*\). We combine the IVW, MR-Egger, and SMR \(p\)-values using an equally weighted Cauchy combination test. We refer to these four MR methods as causal IVW, causal Egger, causal SMR, and causal joint, respectively.

\noindent We applied the framework to cell-type-specific caQTL and eQTL summary statistics from the Chinese Immune Multi-Omics Atlas (CIMA), which profiled circulating immune cells from 428 healthy Chinese adults using scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq \cite{yin2026cima}. We analyzed naive B cells (Bn), naive CD4$^+$ T cells (CD4), and naive CD8$^+$ T cells (CD8).

\noindent For comparison, we also calculated two marginal measures of peak--gene association (Fig.~1A). The marginal multiomics analysis calculated Spearman correlations between peak accessibility and gene expression across weighted nearest-neighbor (WNN) metacells spanning multiple cell types in an external peripheral blood mononuclear cell dataset. The marginal pseudobulk analysis calculated the same correlation across CIMA donors using pseudobulk profiles constructed separately for each cell type. The methods represent common tools that use Spearman's correlations to identify peak-gene pairs, such as Signac, ArchR, and scMEGA \cite{stuart2021signac,granja2021archr,li2023scmega}. 


\subsection{Concordance and discordance between MR estimates and marginal correlations}
\label{res:examples}

Within each cell type, we applied all six methods to the same set of candidate peak--gene pairs (40,087 in naive B cells, 85,527 in naive CD4 T cells, and 50,931 in naive CD8 T cells; \nameref{S3_Table}). Using a BH-adjusted \(p\)-value threshold of 0.1, the marginal multiomics analysis identified 37,181 links among the CD4 T-cell candidate pairs, including 24,632 detected by this method alone. In comparison, each MR-based test identified fewer links, although some links were detected only by MR (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}B). We next examined whether the methods agreed on the direction of the association for the 1,259 CD4 T-cell links that were significant by all six methods. The causal IVW, causal Egger, and causal SMR estimates agreed in sign for 1,258 of these links, indicating the high consistency across MR methods. The causal IVW estimate agreed in sign with both marginal correlations for 918 links (72.9\%). For 304 links, only the metacell correlation differed in sign from the IVW estimate, compared with 17 links for which only the pseudobulk correlation differed. For the remaining 20 links, both correlations had the opposite sign to the IVW estimate (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}C). We observed similar patterns in naive B cells and naive CD8 T cells (\nameref{S3_Fig}).

\noindent Four examples illustrated these patterns. First, for the peak at chr11:117185715--117186216 and \textit{SIDT2} in naive B cells, all six tests were significant, and the three MR estimates and both marginal correlations were positive (Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}A). Next, for \textit{RPL11} and the peak at chr1:23691494--23691995 in naive CD4 T cells, the MR tests and the metacell correlation were also significant, but the donor-level pseudobulk correlation was weak and did not reach significance (Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}B). The \textit{ERAP2} example illustrated a difference in direction. For the peak at chr5:96885894--96886395, all three MR estimates in naive CD4 T cells were negative, as was the correlation across donors (\(\rho=-0.641\), \(p=2.0\times10^{-44}\)). In contrast, the correlation across metacells was positive (\(\rho=0.375\), \(p=6.1\times10^{-4}\)) (Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}C). Finally, for \textit{ARPC1B} and the peak at chr7:99376204--99376705 in naive CD4 T cells, all four MR-based tests were significant and all three MR estimates were positive, but neither marginal correlation reached significance (Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}D). One possible explanation for the discordance is that marginal correlations can reflect shared variation in accessibility and expression across cell types or states \cite{su2025scmultimap,calderon2019landscape}. For example, if both peak accessibility and gene expression are higher in one cell type than in another, pooling metacells across these types can yield a positive correlation even when the association within a given cell type is weak or negative \cite{su2025scmultimap}. Within a cell type, differences in immune activation across donors could likewise influence both measurements and contribute to the pseudobulk correlation \cite{calderon2019landscape}. Thus, a marginal correlation alone cannot establish that accessibility at a candidate enhancer causally affects gene expression. By using genetic variants as instruments, MR methods provide complementary evidence about the causal effects and can identify candidate regulatory links even when marginal correlations are weak, as illustrated by \textit{ARPC1B} (Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}D).

\subsection{Biological evidence for MR-inferred enhancer--gene links}

\noindent To extend the comparison beyond the individual loci in Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}, we compared the genome-wide significance patterns of the causal joint test and the two marginal analyses. The Manhattan plots for the 85,527 candidate peak--gene pairs in naive CD4 T cells show that some pairs with strong MR evidence had little evidence of association in either marginal analysis (Fig.~\ref{fig:go}A, with additional results in Fig.~S7). We therefore examined the functions of genes involved in MR-only links, defined as peak--gene pairs that were significant in at least one MR-based test and in neither marginal analysis.

\noindent We examined the biological functions of genes implicated by MR-only links. Pooling these links across Bn, CD4 T, and CD8 T cells yielded 4,509 unique genes, for which we performed Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis using DAVID \cite{sherman2022david} (Fig.~\ref{fig:go}B). DNA-templated transcription was the most significantly enriched biological process (689 genes, 1.36-fold enrichment, BH-adjusted \(p=5.20\times10^{-18}\)). Regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II was also enriched (415 genes, 1.26-fold enrichment, BH-adjusted \(p=4.70\times10^{-5}\)). RNA processing emerged as a second theme across the biological process and molecular function categories. RNA splicing was enriched among biological processes (84 genes, 1.69-fold enrichment, BH-adjusted \(p=3.39\times10^{-4}\)), while RNA binding and mRNA binding were enriched among molecular functions. Both \textit{SRSF7} and \textit{DDX5} were annotated to all three terms. These findings were relevant to early changes in RNA processing in lymphocytes. For example, human naive B cells in secondary lymphoid tissue exhibited less intron retention than those in peripheral blood, even before activation \cite{ogrady2022splicing}. Changes in alternative splicing had also been observed within six hours of stimulating naive CD4 T cells, with similar overall patterns reported in CD8 T cells \cite{zisman2025splicing}. Protein ubiquitination was likewise enriched (172 genes, 1.50-fold enrichment, BH-adjusted \(p=1.25\times10^{-5}\)), as was the corresponding molecular function, ubiquitin-protein transferase activity (92 genes, 1.66-fold enrichment, BH-adjusted \(p=1.55\times10^{-4}\)). Both terms included \textit{UBE2N}, which encodes the ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme Ubc13 \cite{yamamoto2006ubc13}.

\noindent Peak--gene links identified by MR generally showed stronger enrichment in the CIMA gene regulatory network than those identified by the two marginal methods. To quantify this enrichment, we extracted unique region--gene pairs from the enhancer-driven regulons (eRegulons) that CIMA inferred using SCENIC+ \cite{yin2026cima}. For each method and cell type, we calculated an odds ratio comparing the odds of eRegulon membership among significant peak--gene pairs with the corresponding odds among the remaining tested pairs (see Methods). Although the marginal methods generally identified more links (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}B and \nameref{S3_Fig}), the MR methods yielded higher odds ratios, particularly for the high-quality eRegulon subset (Fig.~\ref{fig:go}D, \nameref{S8_Fig}). These results provided additional support for the regulatory relevance of MR-identified peak--gene links.

\noindent The enhancer-gene linkage inferred only by MR methods shown enrichment across six external regulatory references. The six orthogonal data included GTEx whole-blood eQTLs, EnhancerAtlas, FANTOM5, 4DGenome, H3K27ac HiChIP, and promoter capture Hi-C. We first assessed each of the six methods separately within each cell type, comparing the proportion of significant links supported by each reference with the corresponding proportion among candidate links. All six methods showed enrichment, but causal SMR had the largest fold enrichment in 17 of the 18 combinations of cell type and reference (Tables~S1 and~S2). Moreover, we focused on MR-only links, which was significant in at least one of MR method (causal IVW, causal Egger, and causal SMR) and insignificant for both marginal methods. For each gene, we took the union of its MR-only peaks across the three cell types and, separately, the union of its candidate peaks. Because reference coverage varied among genes, we divided genes into eight equally sized groups according to the proportion of their candidate peaks overlapping the gene's reference regions. Within each group, we compared this pooled background proportion with the pooled overlap proportion for MR-only peaks (Fig 3C, Fig.~S5). For H3K27ac HiChIP, the overlap proportion was generally higher for MR-only peaks across the groups (Fig.~\ref{fig:go}C). Overall, MR-only peaks were 3.28 times as likely as candidate peaks to overlap a HiChIP contact region assigned to the same gene (pooled hypergeometric \(p=6.4\times10^{-620}\)). Thus, MR identified candidate regulatory links with independent chromatin-contact support even when neither marginal correlation reached significance.

\subsection{Causal enhancer–gene links provide insights into disease mechanisms}

To assess the disease relevance of MR-supported peak--gene links, we evaluated heritability enrichment across seven immune-mediated diseases: ankylosing spondylitis (AS), Crohn's disease (CD), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), multiple sclerosis (MS), and type 1 diabetes (T1D) (\nameref{S4_Table}). We hypothesized that peaks involved in MR-only links would capture disease-relevant regulatory signals missed by marginal correlation analyses. We therefore pooled these peaks across the three cell types and estimated their heritability enrichment using stratified LD score regression (S-LDSC) \cite{finucane2015sldsc}, controlling for 97 baseline annotations, with all candidate peaks as a comparison. Peaks involved in MR-only links showed higher heritability enrichment than all candidate peaks across all seven diseases (Fig.~\ref{fig:disease}A), with enrichment $P<0.1$ for CD, IBD, MS, and T1D.


\noindent We next showed three examples illustrating how identified causal enhancer-gene linkages can help identify candidate target genes and cellular contexts of disease-associated variants. Although GWAS have identified many loci associated with immune-mediated diseases, the mechanisms through which these loci act and the relevant cell types often remain unclear \cite{gallagher2018postgwas}. All three examples were MR-only links, detected by MR methods but not by either marginal method. In the first example, causal methods linked the chromatin accessibility peak at chr5:96,896,259--96,896,760 (hg38), which contains the CD-associated variant rs2549785, to \textit{ERAP1} in Bn cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:disease}B). The 5q15 region harboring \textit{ERAP1} is a known CD-associated locus \cite{franke2010cd}. \textit{ERAP1} encodes an endoplasmic reticulum aminopeptidase that trims peptides for presentation by MHC class I molecules \cite{hattori2013erap}, and immune-mediated disease-associated variants at 5q15 affect its transcript and protein expression \cite{hanson2018erap}. In mice with DSS-induced colitis, ERAP1 deficiency increased colonic proinflammatory cytokine levels \cite{blake2022erap1}. Independent regulatory annotations also supported the peak--gene link: the peak overlaps an enhancer predicted to target \textit{ERAP1} by EnhancerAtlas \cite{gao2020enhanceratlas}, and naive B-cell H3K27ac HiChIP data identified a contact between the peak and the \textit{ERAP1} promoter ($Q=8.4\times10^{-3}$) \cite{chandra2021pieqtl}. RegulomeDB further annotated rs2549785 as overlapping a transcription factor binding site or an accessible chromatin region \cite{boyle2012regulomedb,dong2023regulomedb}. Our causal framework nominated \textit{ERAP1} as a target of this peak in Bn cells (beta later) and, and higher peak accessibility was estimated to decrease \textit{ERAP1} expression (beta later). This implied that rs2549785 may contribute to CD risk by altering the accessibility of this peak and, consequently, the expression of \textit{ERAP1} in Bn cells.

\noindent The second example linked a peak containing the T1D-associated variant rs705704 at chr12:56,041,553--56,042,054 (hg38) to \textit{ERBB3} in CD4 T cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:disease}B). This variant lies within the well-established T1D-associated locus at 12q13 \cite{todd2007t1d,hakonarson2008t1d}. Previous work has connected genetic variation at this locus to \textit{ERBB3} expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells from patients with T1D and controls, with protective genotypes associated with higher expression. The same study showed that ERBB3 modulates antigen-presenting cell function in dendritic cells and monocytes \cite{wang2010erbb3}. Functional annotations further suggest that rs705704 lies in a regulatory region: its FAVOR aPC-TF and aPC-EpiActive scores were 29.1 and 18.1, placing it approximately in the top 0.1\% and 1.5\% of variants genome-wide, respectively \cite{zhou2023favor}, and RegulomeDB annotated it as overlapping a transcription factor binding site or an accessible chromatin region \cite{boyle2012regulomedb,dong2023regulomedb}. These findings connect the locus to \textit{ERBB3} and support the regulatory potential of rs705704. Our MR analysis adds a potential cellular context by linking accessibility of the variant-containing peak to \textit{ERBB3} expression in CD4 T cells, suggesting a candidate regulatory mechanism at this T1D-associated locus. The third example linked a peak containing the IBD-associated variant rs2990245 at chr1:155,227,359--155,227,860 (hg38) to \textit{THBS3} in CD8 T cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:disease}B). Previous integration of blood eQTL and IBD GWAS data prioritized \textit{THBS3} as a candidate IBD target gene through SMR and colocalization (posterior probability $0.96$), with the colocalization replicated in FinnGen \cite{gu2025ibd}. Its elevated expression in gene expression datasets from patients with IBD provides additional disease-related context \cite{zhang2025thbs3}. Regulatory evidence supports a connection between the variant-containing region and \textit{THBS3}: rs2990245 lies 18.6 kb from the \textit{THBS3} TSS and is associated with its expression in GTEx whole blood ($P=5.2\times10^{-33}$) \cite{gtex2017genetic}, while naive CD8 T-cell H3K27ac HiChIP data identified a contact between the 5-kb bin containing the peak and the \textit{THBS3} promoter ($Q=1.9\times10^{-48}$) \cite{chandra2021pieqtl}. FAVOR and RegulomeDB also support the regulatory potential of rs2990245, with an aPC-TF score of 21.4 (top 0.7\%)\cite{zhou2023favor,boyle2012regulomedb}. Our MR analysis extends this evidence by estimating the effect of peak accessibility on \textit{THBS3} expression specifically in naive CD8 T cells. Higher peak accessibility was estimated to decrease \textit{THBS3} expression. Neither marginal method detected this link (BH-adjusted $P=0.067$ and $0.45$ for marginal multiomics and pseudobulk). Together, these findings suggest a potential mechanism connecting the IBD-associated variant to altered \textit{THBS3} regulation in naive CD8 T cells.

\subsection{Motif enrichment highlights candidate regulators of MR-supported peak–gene links}
To characterize the regulatory sequence features of the peaks of each method, we performed transcription factor motif enrichment analysis (see Methods). For each cell type and method, the peaks of links with $\q<0.1$ formed the foreground set, and the background was 50,000 candidate peaks matched for GC content and chromatin accessibility. We focused on MR-specific motifs, which were enriched for at least one of causal IVW, causal Egger and causal SMR but for neither marginal method; there were 47 such motifs (23 in Bn cells, 14 in CD4 T cells, and 10 in CD8 T cells). Table~\ref{tab:motif} shows the most significant MR-specific motifs for each cell type. All six motifs in Table~\ref{tab:motif} were enriched for causal Egger, and four of them (FOSL2::JUND, MNT, POU3F1 and TEF) were enriched only for causal Egger; causal Egger estimates depend on the allele orientation of the instruments (see Methods; \nameref{S2_Fig}).

\noindent In CD4 T cells, the MNT motif had the strongest enrichment (motif-level adjusted $P = 0.003$, fold enrichment 1.34 for causal Egger); loss of MNT has been reported in malignant CD4\(^+\) T cells from patients with S\'{e}zary syndrome~\cite{vermeer2008mnt}. In Bn cells, ATF3 had the strongest enrichment, and causal Egger-selected peaks were also enriched for the FOSL2::JUND motif, and JunD, one of its two AP-1 components, is upregulated in B cells from patients with SLE~\cite{wu2024jund}. Other selected motifs included CENPB in CD4 T cells, and POU3F1 and TEF in CD8 T cells (Table~\ref{tab:motif}).
