\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Study overview and comparison of peak–gene linking methods}
\label{res:analysis-set}
We applied MR methods to data from the Chinese Immune Multi-Omics Atlas (CIMA) \cite{yin2026cima}, a multi-omics analysis of 428 healthy Chinese adults that combined scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq of circulating immune cells with whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of plasma cell-free DNA. CIMA applied TensorQTL [CITE: TensorQTL] to identify cell-type-specific \textit{cis}-xQTLs within $\pm$1 Mb of gene transcription start sites (TSSs) or chromatin peak midpoints. We used the cell-type-specific caQTL and eQTL summary statistics to estimate the effect of peak accessibility on gene expression in naive B cells (Bn cells), naive CD4$^+$ T cells (CD4 T cells), and naive CD8$^+$ T cells (CD8 T cells). MR estimates causal effects under the instrumental-variable assumptions, whereas correlation-based methods estimate associations. We used three established MR methods \cite{burgess2016combining,bowden2015egger,burgess2017interpreting,zhu2016smr}, which we call causal IVW, causal Egger and causal SMR, and an equal-weight Cauchy combination of these three tests (causal joint) \cite{liu2020cauchy}. We compared them with two correlation-based methods: Spearman correlation across individual-level pseudobulk profiles of CIMA individuals (marginal pseudobulk; 335 individuals for Bn cells, 372 for CD4 T cells, and 346 for CD8 T cells) and Spearman correlation across 80 WNN metacells of an external PBMC multiome dataset (marginal multiomics) (Fig~\ref{fig:overview}A). We refer to the four MR-based tests (causal IVW, causal Egger, causal SMR and causal joint) collectively as the causal methods, and to the two correlation-based methods as the marginal methods. Significance was defined by the BH-adjusted $P$ value computed within each gene, separately for each method ($\q<0.1$), and an MR-only link is a pair that is significant for at least one of causal IVW, causal Egger and causal SMR but for neither marginal method (see Methods).

\noindent All six methods were applied to the same set of candidate peak--gene pairs (40,087 for Bn cells, 85,527 for CD4 T cells, and 50,931 for CD8 T cells; \nameref{S3_Table}) for a fair comparison. Marginal multiomics found 37,181 links ($\q<0.1$) in CD4 T cells, many more than the other methods. Causal methods (causal IVW, causal Egger, and causal SMR) generally found fewer links than the two marginal methods (marginal multiomics and marginal pseudobulk). The sets of links overlapped between methods (Fig~\ref{fig:overview}B). The results for Bn cells and CD8 T cells were similar (\nameref{S3_Fig}).

\noindent Even if some peak-gene pairs are significant in both marginal and causal methods, the directions might be different. We extracted all 1,259 links in CD4 T cells that were significant for all six methods and compared their effect directions. The results of causal IVW, causal Egger, and causal SMR were highly concordant (1,258 of 1,259 links with the same sign). For 73\% of these links, causal IVW had the same sign as both marginal methods, whereas for 24.1\% the sign of causal IVW differed from that of marginal multiomics only. Marginal multiomics was more likely to be discordant with causal IVW than marginal pseudobulk (Fig~\ref{fig:overview}C, \nameref{S3_Fig}).

\subsection{Concordance and discordance between MR estimates and marginal correlations}
\label{res:examples}
\noindent Mendelian randomization estimates the causal effect of peak accessibility on gene expression, while marginal multiomics and marginal pseudobulk calculate the correlation between peaks and genes across metacells (spanning cell types) and across individuals, respectively. The causal effects and marginal associations agree on many peak-gene pairs, which is reasonable. But discordance still exsits. We select four representative case to illustrate this.

\noindent For the gene \textit{SIDT2} and the peak chr11:117185715-117186216 in Bn cells, the MR results (causal SMR, causal IVW, causal Egger, and causal joint) and two marginal correlations (marginal multiomics and marginal pseudobulk) were all positive and significant ($\q<0.1$) (Fig~\ref{fig:examples}A). Causal IVW gave the smallest $P$ value, and the two lines were closely aligned implying no clear pleiotropy in the data (Fig~\ref{fig:examples}A). \TODO{R2-P2: delete the ``no clear pleiotropy'' clause, or compute the Egger intercept (author to decide).} However, the concordance of significance did not always exist among methods. For second examples (gene \textit{RPL11}, peak chr1:23691494-23691995) in CD4 T cells, while the MR estimates and the correlation across metacells are significant, the correlation across individuals was not significant anymore (Fig~\ref{fig:examples}B). Therefore, the causal effects were not always mirrored by a marginal association within the same cell type.

\noindent The causal effects and the marginal associations might have even different sign. For the gene \textit{ERAP2} and the peak chr5:96885894-96886395 in CD4 T cells, all of causal IVW, causal Egger, and causal SMR detect negative and significant effects, consistent with the negative correlation across individuals ($\rho = -0.641$) (Fig~\ref{fig:examples}C). However, the correlation across cell types was surprisingly positive ($\rho=0.375$, $P=6.1\times10^{-4}$), indicating the possible directional discrepancy between causal effects and marginal association.

\noindent Some links were detected only by MR methods. For example, all MR methods detected a significantly positive effect of the peak chr7:99376204-99376705 on \textit{ARPC1B} in CD4 T cells, whereas neither marginal correlation test provided evidence of association (Fig~\ref{fig:examples}D).

\noindent Together, these examples show the consistency and possible difference between marginal correlation and causal effects. Because marginal multiomics and marginal pseudobulk only calculate the marginal association, which might be influenced by potential unobserved counfunding factors, the calculated correlation may be different or even of different signs from the causal effects. This demonstrates that when studying the regulation mechanism between peak accessibility and gene expression, we should not only study the marginal association, but also find the underlying causal effects.

\subsection{Functional characterization and regulatory network support for peak–gene links}
\TODO{Fig~\ref{fig:go}A (Miami plot, CD4 T cells) and Fig~\ref{fig:go}D (HiChIP overlap of MR-only links; \nameref{S5_Fig}) are not yet cited in the text; \nameref{S7_1_Fig}--\nameref{S7_3_Fig} (genome-wide Manhattan plots) is not yet cited either (author to write).}

To characterize the biological functions of MR-only links (see Methods), we performed GO enrichment analysis using DAVID [CITE: DAVID] on their genes. We pooled the MR-only links from the three cell types, giving 4,509 MR-only genes (4,031 with a GO Biological Process annotation). DNA-templated transcription was the most significant biological process term, including 689 genes, with a fold enrichment of 1.36 and a DAVID-reported FDR of $5.18\times10^{-18}$ (Fig~\ref{fig:go}B). The molecular function enrichment showed protein binding (fold enrichment 1.16, DAVID FDR $2.81 \times 10^{-85}$), RNA binding (fold enrichment 1.41, DAVID FDR $3.58 \times 10^{-15}$), and zinc ion binding (fold enrichment 1.33, DAVID FDR $3.58 \times 10^{-15}$) as the most significant terms (Fig~\ref{fig:go}C). Genes related with the RNA-binding enrichment, such as \textit{SRSF7} amd \textit{DDX5}, were known to have establilshed role in pre-mRNA splicing and RNA processing [CITE: SRSF7 and DDX5 in pre-mRNA splicing and RNA processing]. Genes included in the zinc ion binding term included the zinc-finger proteins \textit{ZNF143} and \textit{IKZF3} [CITE: ZNF143 and IKZF3 as zinc-finger regulators].

\noindent By integrating scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data, CIMA applied SCENIC+ (version 1.0.1) [CITE: SCENIC+] to construct cell-type specific enhancer-driven regulons (eRegulons) and further selected a subset of high-quality eRegulons \cite{yin2026cima}. Each eRegulon is a triple of (TF, chromatin peak, gene), which can reasonably serve as validation data. We extracted unique region-gene pairs from the CIMA eRegulons and calculated the odds ratio for overlap with the links ($\q<0.05$) identified by each method (Fig~\ref{fig:go}E for CD4 T cells, \nameref{S8_Fig} for Bn cells and CD8 T cells; see Methods). Although marginal multiomics and marginal pseudobulk tended to find more links (Fig~\ref{fig:overview}B, \nameref{S3_Fig}), causal methods had higher odds ratios than the marginal methods in most comparisons across cell types. Although overlap with the CIMA regulatory network is not a ground truth for causal regulatory effects, it could partially support the reliability of MR estimates.

\noindent We also tested whether the significant peaks of each method were enriched for peaks overlapping six external regulatory references: EnhancerAtlas, FANTOM5, 4DGenome, GTEx whole-blood eQTLs, H3K27ac HiChIP and promoter capture Hi-C (PCHi-C) (see Methods; \nameref{S1_Table} and \nameref{S2_Table}). The significant peaks of all six methods were enriched for every reference in every cell type (pooled hypergeometric $P<10^{-16}$; fold enrichment 1.68--6.18). Causal SMR had the largest pooled fold enrichment in 17 of the 18 combinations of cell type and reference; the exception was PCHi-C in CD4 T cells, where causal IVW was largest (2.61 versus 2.55 for causal SMR). Marginal multiomics had the smallest fold enrichment in all 18 combinations. For example, in Bn cells the fold enrichment of causal SMR and marginal multiomics was 6.18 and 3.56 for FANTOM5, and 4.46 and 3.08 for HiChIP. The fold enrichments are descriptive and have no confidence intervals; the hypergeometric $P$ values depend on the number of significant peaks and were often smaller for the marginal methods, which had more significant peaks but lower fold enrichment.

\subsection{Disease heritability enrichment and GWAS overlap of MR-supported peak–gene links}

To explore the disease relevance of MR-only links, we assessed heritability enrichment using stratified LD score regression (S-LDSC) \cite{finucane2015sldsc}. We pooled the peaks of MR-only links (see Methods) from the three cell types and obtained 3,777 unique peaks after liftOver to hg19 (MR-only). We constructed an annotation from these 3,777 peaks and fitted the S-LDSC model together with the 97 baselineLD v2.2 annotations. For comparison, all candidate peaks (All peaks) were analyzed with the same S-LDSC pipeline (Fig~\ref{fig:disease}A). We select the seven GWAS triated studied in OneK1K paper (OneK1K reference). \TODO{R4-P1: delete the OneK1K sentence, or provide its source (author to decide).} Enrichment scores were defined as the ratio of the proportion of heritability explained to the proportion of variants. MR-only peaks had higher enrichment point estimates than all candidate peaks across the seven immune-mediated diseases (Fig~\ref{fig:disease}A). Nominal evidence of enrichment at $P<0.1$ was observed for Crohn's disease, inflammatory bowel disease, multiple sclerosis, and type 1 diabetes (Fig~\ref{fig:disease}A).

\noindent We next showed individual MR-only links whose peak regions contained genome-wide significant variants. These were strong MR-only links, with $\q<0.001$ for at least one causal method and $\q\geq0.05$ for both marginal methods (see Methods). For example, an MR-only peak linked to the gene \textit{ERBB3} in CD4 T cells contained rs705704, a variant significantly associated with type 1 diabetes (Fig~\ref{fig:disease}B). This finding nominated \textit{ERBB3} as a candidate target gene of the GWAS-overlapping regulatory region in CD4 T cells. Additional examples included \textit{RGL2}-linked peaks overlapping type 1 diabetes and rheumatoid arthritis variants, a \textit{POLR2E}-linked peak overlapping Crohn’s disease variants, a \textit{GNAS}-linked peak overlapping inflammatory bowel disease and Crohn's disease variants, and an \textit{ERAP1}-linked peak in Bn cells overlapping a Crohn's disease variant (Fig~\ref{fig:disease}B). Although we cannot directly say there were causal relationship between the genes and GWAS diseases, these MR-supported peak-gene links provided a potential regulation mechenism ways for these diseases.

\subsection{Motif enrichment highlights candidate regulators of MR-supported peak–gene links}
To characterize the regulatory sequence features of the peaks of each method, we performed transcription factor motif enrichment analysis (see Methods). For each cell type and method, the peaks of links with $\q<0.1$ formed the foreground set, and the background was 50,000 candidate peaks matched for GC content and chromatin accessibility. We focused on MR-specific motifs, which were enriched for at least one of causal IVW, causal Egger and causal SMR but for neither marginal method; there were 47 such motifs (23 in Bn cells, 14 in CD4 T cells, and 10 in CD8 T cells). Table~\ref{tab:motif} shows the most significant MR-specific motifs for each cell type. All six motifs in Table~\ref{tab:motif} were enriched for causal Egger, and four of them (FOSL2::JUND, MNT, POU3F1 and TEF) were enriched only for causal Egger; causal Egger estimates depend on the allele orientation of the instruments (see Methods; \nameref{S2_Fig}).

\noindent In CD4 T cells, the MNT motif had the strongest enrichment (motif-level adjusted $P = 0.003$, fold enrichment 1.34 for causal Egger); loss of MNT has been reported in malignant CD4\(^+\) T cells from patients with S\'{e}zary syndrome~\cite{vermeer2008mnt}. In Bn cells, ATF3 had the strongest enrichment, and causal Egger-selected peaks were also enriched for the FOSL2::JUND motif, and JunD, one of its two AP-1 components, is upregulated in B cells from patients with SLE~\cite{wu2024jund}. Other selected motifs included CENPB in CD4 T cells, and POU3F1 and TEF in CD8 T cells (Table~\ref{tab:motif}).
